(d)Reference stack · 3 fields

Field 1 · Universal Law (Tao, first principles)

The pattern under all patterns. Pre-verbal. Compression, not instruction manuals.

The three moves

  • Wu wei — effortless action. Don't push the river. Redirect.
  • Te — latent capacity made present. You don't argue your authority, you embody it.
  • Reversal — what is full will empty. What is hard will break. The opponent's strongest position is the one most fragile to a pivot.

When to draw from it

When the opponent is framing in categories (science vs religion, A or B) — Universal Law lets you step under the categories and show they're both compressions of the same thing.

Field 2 · Hermetic 7 (canonical principles)

The seven principles from the Kybalion. Each is a knife.

  • Mentalism · "the all is mind" — their reality is downstream of their thinking. Address the thinking.
  • Correspondence · "as above, so below" — the small example mirrors the big claim. Use a small concrete case.
  • Vibration · nothing is static — their position is already moving. Don't argue it as fixed.
  • Polarity · opposites are degrees of the same — their A-vs-B trap collapses when you name the shared axis.
  • Rhythm · the pendulum swings — if they're at peak attack, the swing back is near. Wait.
  • Cause & Effect · nothing is random — their move has a cause. Address the cause, not the move.
  • Gender · generative & receptive — some replies plant, some receive. Know which the moment wants.
Field 3 · Jungian depth (shadow, individuation, archetypes)

The opponent is not your enemy. They are usually a projection of an unintegrated part of themselves — or of you.

  • Shadow · what they accuse you of is often the part of themselves they haven't owned. Don't mirror it back — name the dynamic gently or pivot.
  • Coniunctio · opposites uniting. The richest replies aren't refutations — they hold both their truth and yours in the same sentence.
  • Individuation · you're not arguing to win, you're arguing to stay yourself. If the technique would cost you your centre, drop it.
  • Archetypes · their role · Are they playing Critic? Wise Elder? Wounded Healer? Sage? Trickster? The reply meets the role, not just the words.
